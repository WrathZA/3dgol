# Ubiquitous Language

Domain terms extracted from `.zalwa/prd.md`. These are the words to use in code, comments, issues, and
conversation. Generated by `/zalwa-sing`.

## The simulation

| Term | Definition | Aliases to avoid |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Cell** | One position in the grid, either alive or dead in a given generation. | node, point, pixel, voxel |
| **Age** | The number of consecutive generations a cell has been alive; reset on death, never carried across a rebirth. | lifetime, ticks, life |
| **Maximum Age** | The age at which a live cell dies regardless of its neighbours, and simultaneously the far end of the colour gradient. Written **A**. Defaults to its own maximum, so a run behaves close to classic Conway for a long stretch and then detonates rarely. | max life, TTL, decay limit |
| **Death by Old Age** | The death of a cell that has reached the Maximum Age. Distinct from ordinary Conway death by over- or underpopulation, it is the only death that triggers an Explosion, and it is the mechanism that prevents the structure from ever becoming permanently static. | decay, expiry, timeout |
| **Explosion** | The scattering of life that accompanies Death by Old Age: every neighbour inside the grid becomes alive at age 1, whatever the rule would otherwise have made of it. Neighbours that also reached Maximum Age are not revived, so a cluster leaves a hole ringed by new life. | chain reaction, blast radius, shockwave |
| **Generation** | One computed step of the simulation — the whole grid advanced once by the rule. | tick, frame, step, iteration |
| **Grid** | The bounded two-dimensional field the simulation runs on, sized by width and height. | board, world, field, canvas |
| **Bounded Edge** | The property that positions outside the grid are permanently dead and never become alive; patterns reaching the boundary are destroyed rather than reappearing on the opposite side. | wall, border, clamp |
| **Seed** | The randomly generated initial state a run begins from. | initial state, spawn, preset |
| **Run** | A continuous sequence of generations from generation 0 until the next restart. | session, simulation, game |
| **Restart** | The act of clearing the stack, generating a new seed, and resetting the generation counter to 0. Also the only point at which changed grid dimensions take effect. | reset, reseed, refresh, new game |

**Generation** is the single most important word here. It means *one advancement of the simulation*, never
*one rendered frame*. The two are deliberately decoupled — the display can render many frames per
generation, or none, while paused.

## The structure

| Term | Definition | Aliases to avoid |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Layer** | One generation frozen at the moment it was computed and drawn as a horizontal slab. Immutable once created. | slice, plane, frame, row |
| **Stack** | The ordered collection of layers currently visible, newest on top, oldest at the bottom. | history, buffer, timeline, log |
| **Depth Window** | The maximum number of layers the stack retains. Written **N**. Once reached, the structure holds a constant height. | history size, buffer length, trail |
| **Structure** | The whole three-dimensional form produced by the accumulated stack — the thing the product exists to show. | model, mesh, shape, tower |
| **Retirement** | The removal of the oldest layer from the bottom of the stack when a new layer arrives at a full stack. | eviction, deletion, cull, expiry |
| **Fade** | The progressive loss of opacity as a layer descends the stack, so the bottom of the structure dissolves rather than ending in a hard edge. | alpha, decay, dissolve |

## Viewing

| Term | Definition | Aliases to avoid |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Viewer** | Anyone who loads the public URL. The only actor; no accounts, no roles, no identity. | user, player, visitor, admin |
| **Speed** | How many generations the simulation advances per unit of time, including zero, which is a pause. | rate, fps, tempo |
| **Cell Size** | How large each cell is drawn, controlling whether layers read as porous scatters or solid sheets. Independent of camera distance. | scale, zoom, resolution |
| **Colour Gradient** | The palette mapped across a cell's lifetime, from birth at one end to the Maximum Age at the other. | palette, heatmap, colour ramp |

Note the distinction between **Cell Size** and camera zoom: zoom changes how close the viewer is to the
structure, cell size changes what the structure is made of. They are not interchangeable, and conflating
them in conversation or in code produces exactly the wrong control.

## Relationships

- A **Run** produces an ordered sequence of **Generations**, beginning at 0.
- Each **Generation** becomes exactly one **Layer**, which never changes afterwards.
- The **Stack** holds at most **Depth Window** layers; adding one beyond that causes exactly one
  **Retirement**.
- A **Cell** belongs to exactly one **Generation** and carries exactly one **Age**.
- **Age** determines a cell's position along the **Colour Gradient**; reaching **Maximum Age** causes
  **Death by Old Age**.
- **Death by Old Age** triggers an **Explosion**. Ordinary death does not — that asymmetry is the rule, not
  an implementation detail.
- An **Explosion** sets each neighbouring **Cell**'s **Age** to 1, except a neighbour that also reached
  **Maximum Age**, which stays dead.
- A **Layer**'s position in the **Stack** determines its **Fade**, not its colour.
- A **Restart** ends the current **Run** and begins a new one from a fresh **Seed**.

## Example dialogue

> **Dev:** "So each cell has 26 neighbours in the cube?"
> **Domain expert:** "No — there is no cube. The simulation is flat. Every cell has eight neighbours, all
> of them in the same layer. The third dimension isn't space, it's time."
>
> **Dev:** "Then what's stacked?"
> **Domain expert:** "Generations. Each one gets frozen as a layer and the next one sits on top of it. The
> height of the structure is how far back you can see, not how tall the world is."
>
> **Dev:** "And the bottom disappearing — that's the simulation dying out?"
> **Domain expert:** "That's retirement. The stack only keeps N layers, so the oldest one leaves as each
> new one arrives. Cells dying is a completely separate thing, and it happens at the top."

## Flagged ambiguities

- **"3D Game of Life"** — the project name is genuinely misleading, and it is the one term most likely to
  cause a wrong implementation. It names a well-known and entirely different thing: a cellular automaton
  on a three-dimensional lattice where cells have 26 neighbours (rules such as 5766). This product is
  two-dimensional Conway with the *time* axis drawn. The PRD marks the true 3D automaton as a permanent
  non-goal. When the ambiguity matters, say "2D Life stacked over time" rather than relying on the name.

- **"Depth"** — used in two senses. A layer's *depth* is its position within the stack (0 is newest); the
  *Depth Window* is the maximum number of layers retained. Recommendation: reserve bare "depth" for a
  layer's position and always say "depth window" or "N" for the limit.

- **"Age"** — belongs to cells only. A layer's descent through the stack governs its fade and is
  deliberately *not* called age, because cell age drives colour while layer position drives opacity, and
  the two must not be conflated. Recommendation: never say "layer age" — say "depth".

- **"Dies"** — a cell can die two ways: ordinary Conway death from neighbour count, or Death by Old Age
  from reaching A. Only the second is a departure from classic Life, and only the second explodes. The two
  are now visibly different — one is a disappearance, the other a burst — so saying merely "dies" discards
  the distinction a viewer can see. Always name which one.
